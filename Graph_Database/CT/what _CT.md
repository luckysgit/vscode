**Counter-terrorism (CT)** means the strategies, operations, and policies used by governments and security organizations to **prevent, detect, disrupt, and respond to terrorism**.

### Main areas of counter-terrorism

1. **Prevention**

   * Identifying potential terrorist threats.
   * Protecting important infrastructure and public places.
   * Preventing recruitment and radicalization.

2. **Intelligence**

   * Collecting and analyzing information about terrorist organizations.
   * Tracking communications, finances, movements, and networks.
   * Sharing intelligence between security agencies.

3. **Disruption**

   * Preventing planned attacks before they happen.
   * Disrupting terrorist financing, logistics, recruitment, and supply networks.

4. **Law enforcement**

   * Investigating terrorist activities.
   * Arresting suspects based on applicable laws and evidence.
   * Prosecuting terrorism-related crimes.

5. **Military operations**

   * In some situations, military forces conduct operations against terrorist groups, particularly in conflict zones.

6. **Cyber counter-terrorism**

   * Monitoring and disrupting terrorist use of online platforms.
   * Detecting terrorist propaganda, recruitment, financing, and coordination activities.

7. **Emergency response**

   * Responding to terrorist attacks.
   * Protecting civilians and critical infrastructure.
   * Coordinating police, military, medical, and emergency services.

### Simple example

Suppose intelligence agencies discover that a terrorist group is planning an attack.

**Intelligence -> threat identification -> investigation -> disruption/arrest -> protection of potential targets -> emergency preparedness**

That entire system of preventing and responding to the threat is broadly called **counter-terrorism**.

It is different from **counterinsurgency (COIN)**. Counter-terrorism primarily focuses on preventing and disrupting terrorism, while counterinsurgency is generally concerned with defeating an organized rebellion or insurgency and addressing the political, military, and social factors supporting it.
