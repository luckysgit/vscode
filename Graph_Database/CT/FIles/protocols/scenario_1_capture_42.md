In network security and malware research, **Scenario 1 (Capture 42)** refers to a specific, widely used network traffic dataset from the **CTU-13 Dataset**, created in 2011 by researchers at the Czech Technical University (CTU) within the Stratosphere Laboratory.

---

### Context: The CTU-13 Dataset

The CTU-13 benchmark was designed to provide realistic, labeled network traffic to train and evaluate intrusion detection systems (IDS), botnet detection algorithms, and anomaly detection models. It consists of 13 different operational scenarios featuring various types of malware running alongside normal campus background traffic.

---

### Specifics of Scenario 1 (Capture 42)

* **Malware Family:** **Neris** (specifically, an IRC-based botnet trojan/worm).
* **Infected Host IP:** `147.32.84.165`
* **Capture Date:** August 10, 2011 (often reflected in filenames such as `capture20110810.binetflow` or `.pcap`).
* **Duration:** Approximately 6.15 hours.
* **Traffic Categories:**
1. **Botnet Traffic:** Traffic generated by the infected host, including Command-and-Control (C2) communications over IRC, port scanning, spam transmission, and remote payload interactions.
2. **Normal Traffic:** Benign traffic generated by verified, non-infected testbed hosts acting as standard internet users.
3. **Background Traffic:** Normal background network activity originating from the broader university network environment.



---

### Data Artifacts Available for Capture 42

Depending on the depth of analysis required, the dataset provides Capture 42 in several formats:

* **Raw PCAP files:** Full packet-level packet captures for deep packet inspection (DPI) and protocol dissection.
* **Bi-directional NetFlow (`.binetflow`):** Pre-aggregated flow records generated by Argus (NetFlow v5/IPFIX style), containing connection 5-tuples, duration, packet/byte counts, TCP state flags, and ground-truth behavioral labels (e.g., `flow=From-Botnet...`, `flow=Background...`).
* **Web/Log Exports:** Standardized logs derived from Zeek/Bro and Argus.

Because Capture 42 clearly isolates an infected C2 host within a high-volume background environment, it is commonly used as a baseline benchmark for testing unsupervised anomaly detection (e.g., Isolation Forest, autoencoders), temporal host profiling, and graph-based threat intelligence pipelines.
